Beyond Accuracy Claims: A Casemix-by-Casemix Blueprint for Autonomous Clinical Coding.

Autonomy Without Evidence Is Risk. Autonomy With Proof Is Transformation.

How HCS v2.0 moves from coder assistance to measured, governed and provable automation.

Validate HCS on Your CasemixSee the Validation Method
The Problem

Unproven autonomy is a funding and revenue assurance risk.

Clinical coding underpins funding and revenue assurance across hospitals. The risk is not limited to one funding model: when automation is introduced at scale, every hospital needs evidence that coding performance is accurate, explainable and controlled before results flow into billing and claiming.

Accuracy must be visibleA single episode-level percentage can conceal where coding risk actually sits.
Performance must be localResults need to be benchmarked against your own coded data and casemix, not a generic benchmark.
Assurance must sit before billingGovernance and audit controls need to operate before a coding result becomes a financial outcome.

01 — Measured

Start with a baseline. Benchmark it on your own coded data.

Rather than leading with a universal accuracy target, HCS v2.0 establishes a baseline position at each coding segment, compares that performance with the hospital's existing human-coded data, and makes the variance visible before tuning begins.

Baseline first: starting accuracy will vary by casemix, documentation quality, document scope and coding segment. The important measure is the evidenced movement from that starting point toward the high-accuracy level agreed for production.
Question answered: At what level is accuracy measured, and against whose coding?

What the validation should show

  • Separate measurement across principal diagnosis, additional diagnoses, procedure coding and final DRG outcome.
  • A baseline compared against the customer's own coded episodes.
  • Segment-by-segment variance that tells the HIM and engineering teams exactly what needs tuning.
  • Clear boundaries around documentation quality, document scope and grouping logic.
02 — Tuned

Tune each coding segment toward an agreed high-accuracy level.

Once the baseline is understood, HCS v2.0 uses HIM and coding expertise to refine the areas where variance sits. Coder corrections are classified at the relevant coding segment, rules are refined, and progress is re-measured against the hospital's own coded outcomes rather than a generic benchmark.

Question answered: What happens to a coder's correction, and how does accuracy improve?

What tuning should make visible

  • Decision-specific logic and specialist coding behaviour.
  • Coder corrections classified by coding segment and cause.
  • Rules and coding guidance maintained with accountable ownership.
  • A repeatable feedback loop between validation, correction and controlled release.
03 — Governed

Controlled and auditable before funding or revenue is affected.

Governance is not an afterthought. HCS v2.0 places visible controls between AI output and the confirmed coding outcome so public and private hospitals can protect funding integrity, revenue assurance and auditability before billing or claiming.

Question answered: What control sits between the confirmed coding outcome and the financial result?

Governance points

  • Health Information Managers own the gold standard used for validation.
  • HIM teams govern the rule library and release gates.
  • Coders remain accountable for validating the documentation, standards and coding outcome.
  • Reasoning and supporting evidence remain visible at validation.
  • Rules and audit controls operate before billing or claiming.
  • Retrospective findings feed back into the assurance process.
04 — Proven

Autonomy is earned, not assumed.

The HCS v2.0 story moves along a maturity curve only after performance is demonstrated first against the hospital's own coding team and then across real production throughput. Failures are not hidden: segments below the agreed threshold, casemix awaiting sufficient evidence, and known limitations remain visible to the governance team.

Questions answered: Against whose coding is it benchmarked? Will you show the failures as well as the results?

Two gates before autonomy

  • Gate 1 — benchmarked accuracy on the same casemix against the client's own coding team.
  • Gate 2 — proof that accuracy and variance remain acceptable at production scale.
  • Failures, exceptions and limitations remain visible rather than averaged away.
  • Complex casemix stays with the coder where evidence does not support autonomy.
  • Straight-through processing is enabled only when the organisation is satisfied with the evidence.
The Assurance Loop

Accuracy is not a one-off test. It is a controlled loop.

Define the measure, apply HIM expertise, engineer the coding decision and close the loop before billing — with the clinical coder validating at the centre.

Autonomy Maturity Curve

Move from assistance to straight-through only where evidence supports it.

Stage 1Coder-Assisted

AI supports the coder while human review remains the primary control.

Stage 2Validated Assistance

Performance is measured and tuned against real coded episodes.

Stage 3Selective Autonomy

Defined casemix areas can move forward under explicit governance gates.

Stage 4Straight-Through

Automation is enabled only where performance has been proven, failures and limitations remain visible, and governance remains satisfied.

Transformation

Redirect coder capacity to the work that needs expert judgement most.

The end-state is not simply fewer touches. It is a different operating model: repeatable casemix can move through validated automation while coder capacity shifts toward complex DRGs, exceptions, audit and higher-value review.
